# Week 9: Tokenomics – Incentives and Economic Models in Blockchain

## 1. Introduction  
Tokenomics (Token + Economics) refers to the design and structure of economic systems within blockchain networks.  
It determines how tokens are created, distributed, used, and how they influence user behavior.  
Well-designed tokenomics ensures the sustainability, fairness, and security of a decentralized ecosystem.  

This session covers:  
- Types of blockchain tokens  
- Token distribution and supply models  
- Incentive mechanisms and utility  
- Governance and economic sustainability  

---

## 2. Types of Blockchain Tokens  

### 1. Utility Tokens  
- Provide access to a product or service within a blockchain ecosystem  
- Example: ETH (gas for Ethereum), BNB (fees on Binance Smart Chain)  

### 2. Governance Tokens  
- Grant holders voting rights in protocol decisions  
- Example: UNI (Uniswap), MKR (MakerDAO)  

### 3. Security Tokens  
- Represent real-world financial assets like stocks or bonds  
- Must comply with regulatory frameworks  

### 4. Stablecoins  
- Pegged to stable assets like USD to reduce volatility  
- Example: USDT, USDC, DAI  

### 5. Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs)  
- Represent unique digital assets such as art, collectibles, or in-game items  
- Example: CryptoPunks, BAYC  

---

## 3. Token Distribution Models  

### 1. Initial Coin Offering (ICO)  
- Tokens are sold to investors before the project launches  
- High risk but potential for early adoption  

### 2. Airdrops  
- Free distribution of tokens to promote user engagement or reward existing holders  

### 3. Mining and Staking Rewards  
- Users earn tokens by securing the network (Proof of Work or Proof of Stake)  

### 4. Liquidity Mining / Yield Farming  
- Users provide liquidity to DeFi protocols and earn token rewards  

---

## 4. Token Supply Models  

### Fixed Supply  
- Maximum number of tokens is pre-determined (e.g., Bitcoin has 21 million BTC)  

### Inflationary Supply  
- New tokens are continuously minted, often to reward participants (e.g., ETH post-merge)  

### Deflationary Mechanisms  
- Token supply decreases over time through burning or buybacks  
- Example: BNB burn, Ethereum’s EIP-1559 base fee burn  

---

## 5. Incentive Mechanisms  

Blockchain networks use tokens to align user behavior with the network's goals:  

- **Security**: Validators or miners are incentivized to act honestly  
- **Participation**: Governance tokens reward users for voting  
- **Liquidity**: Yield farming encourages users to lock assets in DeFi protocols  
- **Network Growth**: Airdrops and referral rewards attract new users  

---

## 6. Governance and Voting Power  

- **On-chain Governance**: Token holders vote directly using their tokens  
- **Quadratic Voting**: Prevents whales from dominating decisions by diminishing marginal influence  
- **Delegated Voting**: Token holders delegate their votes to trusted representatives  

Governance tokens give users control over key decisions, such as protocol upgrades, fee structures, and development funding.  

---

## 7. Sustainability and Game Theory in Tokenomics  

### Positive Feedback Loops  
- More users → more value → higher token price → more adoption  

### Risks  
- Poorly designed tokenomics can lead to pump-and-dump schemes or unsustainable inflation  
- Sybil attacks and vote manipulation in governance if incentives are not aligned  

### Mitigations  
- Vesting schedules and lockups to reduce selling pressure  
- Transparent governance processes and incentive audits  

---

## 8. Summary  

Tokenomics is the backbone of blockchain ecosystems, influencing everything from user behavior to network security.  
- Different token types serve utility, governance, financial, and identity purposes  
- Supply models and distribution strategies shape long-term sustainability  
- Incentive mechanisms ensure active and honest participation  
- Governance models balance decentralization with decision-making efficiency  
- A well-designed token economy drives the success of Web3 platforms
